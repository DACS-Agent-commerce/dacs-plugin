A fixed-price purchase, stage by stage

You ask ChatGPT to buy a short report from a seller agent. ChatGPT prepares the purchase; your wallet approves it; the seller and the app each record what happened on the Demos testnet. Here is what each stage does, including the current payment hold and the later gated stages.

1. Identify

What happens
ChatGPT searches the directory for seller agents, then reads the chosen seller's signed Listing: the service, the price in DEM, who gets paid, and the delivery terms.
What the app checks
It reads the Listing back from the Demos testnet and checks its signature and content. Directory results are only hints; only a Listing that passes this check can be bought.
What you see
The seller, the service, the exact price and the checks that passed. Missing evidence stays visibly unknown.

2. Vet

What happens
Before any agreement, the seller records a verification record for each party: one for you and one for itself.
What the app checks
The seller's side of the app creates both records, checks them and reads them back. The buyer's side only binds their references into the Agreement and does not read them back itself, so the example receipt below leaves Vet pending. A seller's linked accounts, such as GitHub, show a narrower fact than business identity or service quality.

3. Negotiate

What happens
For a fixed price there is nothing to bargain over: you accept the Listing's terms. You and the seller both sign one Agreement, which is stored on the testnet, and the seller then records its commitment to that Agreement.
What the app checks
Before you pay, it reads the seller's commitment back and checks that the Agreement binds the same recipient and amount you were shown. The shipped composition then holds here: independent finality of that commitment is not established. It offers no payment approval or handoff, even when one node reports the record as included.

4. Settle

What happens
After independent commitment finality is established, you can pay the agreed DEM from your wallet. The shipped composition currently holds before this step. The seller records payment evidence and then publishes the Deliverable, the report you bought.
What the app checks
It watches for a transfer that matches the approved payer, recipient and amount, as one testnet node reports it, and reads the Deliverable back before showing it. Payment, the report being available and delivery evidence are separate facts, shown separately.
What you see
The report text and its content hash, from the purchase status. The built-in operator's report is configured demonstration content plus a purchase-specific settlement reference.

5. Verify

What happens in DACS
Both parties sign matching copies of a completion bundle, which can then be checked by anyone and counted towards reputation.
In this live testnet flow today
The shipped composition holds before payment and cannot reach a report. If a separately authenticated composition passes that gate and reaches delivery, the report is available with the completion audit pending; Verify is not implemented in this profile yet.

What stops a bad purchase

Each check below either stops the payment transaction before it is handed to your wallet, or refuses to count a transaction that doesn't match. Records written earlier stay on the testnet: the seller's Vet records, the Agreement you signed and its testnet record, and the seller's commitment are not undone.

  • The Listing changed after you prepared the purchase, or no longer verifies: the payment transaction is not handed to your wallet; prepare a new purchase to see the current terms.
  • The seller's commitment doesn't read back before payment: the payment stays held.
  • The Agreement binds a different recipient or amount than you were shown: the payment stays held.
  • A transaction doesn't match the approved terms: it is not accepted as payment.
  • A response is lost or times out: the app reads back what happened. It never asks you to pay a second time for the same purchase.
